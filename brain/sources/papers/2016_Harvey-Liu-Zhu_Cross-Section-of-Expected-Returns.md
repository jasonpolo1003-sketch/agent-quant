# ...and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns (Harvey, Liu, Zhu)

**Citation:** Harvey, C.R., Liu, Y., Zhu, H. (2016). …and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns. Review of Financial Studies 29(1), 5–68.  
**Free copy:** https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P118_and_the_cross.PDF  
**Version:** Author-hosted (Duke), published RFS version

## Key points (each backed by verbatim quote)

1. **Usual significance criteria fail under data mining**  
   > "Given this extensive data mining, it does not make sense to use the usual criteria for establishing significance." (PDF p.1)

2. **t > 3.0 hurdle**  
   > "A new factor needs to clear a much higher hurdle, with a t-statistic greater than 3.0." (PDF p.1)

3. **Most findings likely false**  
   > "We argue that most claimed research findings in financial economics are likely false." (PDF p.1)

4. **Built on 313 papers**  
   > "We begin with 313 papers published in a selection of journals that study cross-sectional return patterns." (PDF p.2)

## Relevance to Quant Agent Brain
Supports a **multiple-testing gate**: when a rule variant is selected from many, require t > 3 (or DSR) rather than t > 2 before a strategy card can be proposed.
