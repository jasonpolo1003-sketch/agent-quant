# Indexing and Stock Market Serial Dependence Around the World (Baltussen, van Bekkum, Da)

**Citation:** Baltussen, G., van Bekkum, S., Da, Z. (2019). Indexing and stock market serial dependence around the world. Journal of Financial Economics 132(1), 26–48.  
**Free copy:** https://pure.eur.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/47573440/REPUB_113320.pdf  
**Version:** Publisher layout, posted by Erasmus University under Dutch copyright Art. 25fa (personal use)

## Key points (verbatim quotes)

1. **Index autocorrelation flipped from positive to negative**  
   > "While many studies find serial dependence to be positive until the 1990s, it switches to negative since the 20 0 0s." (PDF p.2)

2. **Linked to index products**  
   > "the decline in serial dependence links to the increasing popularity of index products (e.g., futures, exchangetraded funds, and index mutual funds)." (PDF p.2)

3. **ETFs negative since inception**  
   > "serial dependence in the futures and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) covering our equity indexes is negative ever since their inception." (PDF p.3)

4. **Tradable size**  
   > "would result in an annual Sharpe ratio of 0.63 (0.67) after March 2, 1999" (PDF p.3)

## Relevance to Quant Agent Brain
Most direct academic support for *why* daily index ETF mean reversion exists (negative serial dependence tied to indexing/arbitrage).

## RX-0001 flag
Does NOT by itself explain a post-2015 fade (it predicts negative dependence persisting as indexing grows). Test idea: measure SPY/QQQ/IWM daily AR(1) on Tiingo data by year; if it moved toward 0 after 2015 that is a candidate explanation for RX-0001's decay.
