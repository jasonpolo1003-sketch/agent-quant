## Summary
OU z-score rule (60-day window, buy z<-1.25, exit z>-0.5, stop z<-3.5, fills at the signal close, long only) on the five shortest-half-life vs five trending S&P 500 stocks, 40 six-month walk-forward windows 2006-2025. Short half-life arm grew 5.2%/yr vs 3.3% trending (gross); applying 0.1% per side costs afterwards cut the risk-matched version from 9.1% to 5.7%/yr. Half and full Kelly changed little because each stock made about nine trades a year.
## Why it matters
Single-stock, not ETFs, but it gives a documented z-score exit spec, a cost-drag example for frequent mean-reversion trades, and evidence that Kelly sizing is weak with few trades. Authors flag same-close fills, no dividends and survivorship gaps as limits.
